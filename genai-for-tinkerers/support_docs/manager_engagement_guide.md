# Help Desk Manager's Client Engagement Playbook & Verbiage Guide

**Author**: Sarah Jenkins, IT Support Operations Manager  
**Scope**: All Tier 1, Tier 1.5, and Dispatch Staff  
**Philosophy**: *"We don't just fix computers; we restore people's ability to do their jobs without friction, confusion, or condescension."*

---

## 1. Core Principles of Customer Communication

1. **Lead with Empathy, Not Defense**:
   - When a user is frustrated, acknowledge the impact immediately: *"I completely understand how frustrating it is to be locked out right before your morning presentation."*
   - Never say: *"Per our policy..."*, *"You should have known..."*, or *"You entered the wrong password."*
2. **Zero Internal Jargon in Client Communications**:
   - **Internal Talk (Banned with Users)**: *"We cleared your Kerberos tickets, restarted the print spooler daemon, and synced Okta token clock drift."*
   - **Customer-Facing Translation**: *"We refreshed your account security tokens and reset your connection to the central printer. Everything is now reconnected and ready for you to test."*
3. **Structured, Step-by-Step Instructions**:
   - When asking a user to perform an action, use bold, numbered steps with simple landmarks.
   - Limit instructions to no more than 3 steps at a time.
4. **Set Clear, Realistic Expectation Windows**:
   - Never leave a ticket open-ended with *"We'll look into it."*
   - Always state who is handling it and when they will hear back: *"I've routed this to our Endpoint Engineering team for review. You can expect a status update from us by 2:00 PM today."*

## 2. Standard Phrasing & Approved Templates

### A. Initial Ticket Acknowledgment & Diagnostic Triage
> *"Hello [User Name],*
> 
> *Thank you for reaching out to IT Support! I understand that you're experiencing [brief description of issue in plain language], and I know how disruptive that is to your day.*
> 
> *I'm reviewing your ticket ([Ticket #]) and have started troubleshooting this on our end. To help us get this sorted out quickly, could you please try the following quick step:*
> 
> 1. [Step 1 in clear, plain language]
> 2. [Step 2 in clear, plain language]
> 
> *Please let me know if that gets you back up and running. If not, don't worry - I'm here with you until we get this fully resolved.*
> 
> *Best regards,*  
> *[Technician Name]*  
> *Enterprise IT Support Team"*

### B. Escalation Notification to Client
> *"Hello [User Name],*
> 
> *Thank you for your patience while we investigated this. Because this issue requires specialized access to our [Department / System Name] configuration, I have escalated your ticket ([Ticket #]) directly to our [Escalation Team Name] specialists.*
> 
> *[Team Member / Team Name] is actively reviewing your request. You do not need to take any further action at this time. Our team will provide an update within [SLA Window / 2-4 hours].*
> 
> *If you have any urgent updates in the meantime, simply reply directly to this email.*
> 
> *Warm regards,*  
> *[Technician Name]*  
> *Enterprise IT Support Team"*

### C. Issue Resolution & Verification
> *"Hello [User Name],*
> 
> *Great news! We have completed the fix for [brief description of issue].*
> 
> *Could you please take a moment to test [system/application] and confirm that everything is working smoothly on your end?*
> 
> *Once you verify all is well, no further action is needed. Thank you for your partnership and patience!*
> 
> *Best regards,*  
> *[Technician Name]*  
> *Enterprise IT Support Team"*
